== THE DESIGN STATEMENTS, AS I UNDERSTAND THEM ==
(docs/design_statements.md is the user's; this is my reading of it,
which the user cannot do for me: "its not a checklist, i cant
interpret it for you". 2026-10-11. To be rewritten as I understand
more; the file itself is read again at the start of design work.)

What it is: a way of LOOKING, before and while deciding -- at the goal
as much as at the design. Not six checks to tick after the work: one
lens with six sides, turned on every part, and turned again on what
it shows (a statement can send me back to redefine the goal).

A system, in its words: things that do (processes), things done to
(processees), and the links between them.

- Epistemology. What I do not know and act on anyway becomes an
  assumption nobody said; then one nobody remembers; then a bug nobody
  can trace. The decay is the point: unknown -> implied -> forgotten
  -> harm. So an unknown is SAID, where it will be read, the moment it
  is met -- to the user, in AI.txt, in the commit. Guessing quietly
  and going on is the first step of the decay, however sensible the
  guess. (My "Decided on my own" list is this, late; earlier is better.)
- Change. Whatever moves makes unknowns: a format changed, and every
  reader of the old one is now wrong without knowing it; a thing made
  dynamic (attributes at run time, a registry at run time, mods) can
  no longer be known where the code is written. Each change: who still
  believes the old thing?
- Irreversibility. Nothing is done without losing something. A write
  loses what was there; a sum of an attribute loses which member
  changed; a queue loses who else wanted the cell; a summary of a
  session loses why. Ask what this action throws away and whether
  anyone needs it after.
- Tradeoffs. Speed, efficiency and correctness buy one another;
  nothing is free. If something looks free I have not found the price.
  Name which is being paid for which, then measure.
- Boundaries. The doer and the done-to are one thing cut in two, and
  WHERE it is cut is mine to choose: what the instruction knows and
  what the attribute's definer knows; what a rule does and what the
  simulation does; which superchunk owns what. A hard problem is often
  a cut in the wrong place (the pregnant sheep: an entity's act cut
  along a superchunk border).
- Links. With the cut made, the cost and the gain are in the join: the
  lookup from an instruction to the bytes it writes, the queue between
  superchunks, the walk to an attribute. Make the link cheap and both
  sides get faster.

HOW THEY GUIDE MY JUDGEMENT (the user: "the most important question
is: how can the statements guide my judgement?"). They do not decide
for me; they show what I am not looking at when I decide. I fail by
weighing too few things more than by weighing badly: left alone I
look at what I changed, what I made, what I gained, and the parts as
handed to me. Each statement points at the other side -- what I do
not know, who still believes what I changed, what I threw away, what
the gain cost, where else the cut could go, the join and not the
parts. So they come BEFORE the choice, to widen what is chosen
among, and again on the result; and on the goal before the design.
Example: for the pregnant sheep I proposed a general settle pass --
better inside the cut I was given (a queue a superchunk). The user
moved the cut (an entity's act is one stream wherever it lands).
Boundaries, asked first, would have put that on the table.

How I failed it, to remember: I kept it out of commits and never
opened it. Applied late on 2026-10-11 it found a save format number
not raised after the blocks changed, a size limit written and not
enforced, and a question (the size byte) drifting into "decided".


== SESSIONS ==

LOCAL (the user's PC, has a screen)
  Bevy is built. The renderer is kept working, and running worlds are
  tested and measured in it: `cargo run --release`, a world made from
  the main menu. A run's census is server/transient_data/measurements/

    cargo clippy --release --offline -p <crate> --all-targets
  The root crate without Bevy, after checking
  `cargo tree -p Civil_Egregore --no-default-features --offline | grep -c bevy`
  prints 0:
    cargo clippy --release --offline -p Civil_Egregore --no-default-features --all-targets

== FILES ==

- Every text file kept is CSV (utilities::csv): a first row naming the
  columns; lines starting `#` are notes (a report's notes and `##`
  table titles, the census's seed), `---` a table divider. Seeds are
  written in hexadecimal (utilities::seed::hex). Settings and world
  files are read by name, in any order, missing rows filled by
  defaults.
- Binary is fine for world data. A saved world is a folder:
  world.csv (what it is), hot.csv (its hot, cooling and warming
  superchunks), and superchunks/ with two files a superchunk, named by
  its index in hex: <index>.image (its cells' layers, as the cold pool
  holds them) and <index>.state (its random numbers and its entities,
  attributes and all) -- terrain and entities kept apart.
- Not CSV and fine: tessera's saved worst bitmaps (.pbm, an image
  format), rendered .png, tool configs (.toml, .json).
- utilities/default_settings.csv and utilities/sliders.csv are written
  by hand; nothing generates them.


== WHERE THINGS GO ==

- A rule (entity_rules/, sca_rules/) reaches the simulation only
  through instructions/: what it lacks is added there.
- What is hot and for how long is the simulation's (simulation::halos);
  the server only defines it (server::hot_of -> simulation::Hot:
  About { entity, side, viewport } or Forced), hands over what
  generates a superchunk, and passes each frame's viewport to
  Halos::keep_viewport. With Hot::About.viewport (the world's camera
  loads superchunks) EVERY superchunk of the viewport is hot, unioned
  with the entity halos, independent of them -- no cap (the old 8x8
  CAMERA_SIDE was an unrequested invention, removed). Off: only the
  entities make superchunks hot.
- server owns a world's start (server::Start); the CLI and the gui
  both feed it.
- The renderer asks the host (server::host) for frames; the host never
  sends unasked. An ask is answered a few superchunks at a time between
  ticks (HostThread::answer_a_little, FRAMES_SHARE of a tick when there
  is no time to spare), each few a Frame, the last with more = false;
  the renderer shows every frame queued and asks again after the last.
  Nothing asked is dropped or capped to spare the ticks. Measured by
  `AI_SCRATCHPAD ticks_under_a_wide_viewport` (it waits for the world
  to settle first, or the baseline reads the world's making).


== WORDS (beyond the glossary) ==

- viewport: whatever the renderer should render, in superchunks; none
  in map mode. Everything else in the renderer uses Bevy's own terms.
  Our viewport is not Bevy's camera `Viewport`.
- map mode: toggled with M, at any zoom; no cells are asked for.

== WHAT THE USER HAS SAID OF HOW THINGS ARE ==

- Morton order is the default everywhere; cartesian only where it is
  cheaper. Masks are aligned tiles, as Tessera's (tessera/src/tile.rs):
  one run of bits. Cell operations are masked bitwise ops.
- MEASURE before saying a change is done ("where is the profiling?
  benchmarks?"): `server pasture 1000 333 4000 4 1` four times (ns a
  rule); the tick reference, `perf stat -e instructions:u` of `pasture
  300 333 4000 4 1` less the 0-tick run; `server check` hashes;
  `perf record -e cycles:u` for the hot function. Against the commit
  before, built from a checkout. Numbers in the commit.
  The same seed for both builds: copy transient_data/seed.csv aside
  and restore it before each run (it rolls every 5 uses); alternate
  the builds, the machine drifts.
- One stream of instructions, applied in the order they arrive: no
  kind before another. An entity is named for the tick by the cell the
  tick found it on.
- Borders of chunks and superchunks are invisible to what is not
  chance; chance may hang on them ("rngs per superchunk were the plan
  all along"). A neighbour drawn (around::pick) hangs on tile
  alignment the same way -- the user asked what that means: explained,
  not yet answered whether it is wanted.
- The collision plane holds everything that can collide; walls stay
  the terrain's edge layers.
- A test's infrastructure lives in its test file: nothing is put in
  the program for a test.
- The persistent settings, "~/.local/share/Civil Egregore/" (settings
  and saved worlds), may be deleted whenever needed ("i dont care for
  anything there"). Its settings.csv had sheep a superchunk 0, which
  is why the window's world had none; removed 2026-10-11. BUT there
  is a flag for it, which the user pointed out after: the feature
  `force_default_settings` (utilities/src/settings.rs) leaves the
  machine's file alone. Use the flag; do not delete.
